Scope
-----
The accompanying upstream_candidates.v contains the thirteen remaining
candidates, grouped into seven proposed PRs. These are proposals for review,
not a claim that maintainers must accept every convenience lemma. No exact
sequence-based counterpart was found in the checked upstream sources.
All are derivable from existing facts; their value is a reusable interface.
Names and PR groups below are proposed, not assigned GitHub PR numbers.
The six lemmas already prepared in numbered PRs are listed separately.

For each candidate, the audit examined its Prosa declaration and callers,
searched upstream declarations and inspected the closest relevant facts.
Statements use native MathComp notation and statement macros. Proofs use
SSR views, rewriting, induction where useful, and explicit terminators;
no lia, admits, axioms, Prosa imports, or Prosa-specific notation is used.

PR A: singleton and partition big operators over sequences (boot/bigop.v)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. big_pred1_seq
   Prosa: util/bigop.v, same name.
   Upstream: big_pred1/big_pred1_eq require a finType; neither accepts an
   arbitrary unique sequence. filter_pred1_uniq and big_seq1 provide the
   short derivation. This is a sequence convenience companion.
   Need: used by analysis/facts/model/workload.v,
   analysis/facts/model/service_of_jobs.v, and
   results/transfer_schedulability/criterion.v. It also simplifies item 2.
   Statement: arbitrary monoid, eqType indices, sequence membership,
   uniqueness, and extensional equality with pred1. No commutativity needed.
   Proof: eq_bigl, big_filter, filter_pred1_uniq, big_seq1.

2. partition_big_seq
   Prosa: util/sum.v, sum_over_partitions_eq.
   Upstream: partition_big requires finite-type partition labels; its
   label predicate is not an arbitrary unique sequence over an eqType.
   exchange_big supplies the interchange, but not this sequence interface.
   Need: analysis/facts/model/workload.v uses the Prosa equality to group
   work by task. The wrapper avoids repeating interchange and singleton
   proofs in clients.
   Statement: generalized from nat addition to any commutative monoid.
   Only the label sequence must be unique; the input sequence may repeat
   elements. Coverage is restricted to selected elements of that sequence.
   Proof: exchange_big, big_mkcond, eq_big_seq, and item 1.
   Dependency: proposed in the same PR as item 1.

PR B: uniqueness and membership of concatenated sequences (boot/bigop.v)
--------------------------------------------------------
3. bigcat_uniq
   Prosa: util/bigcat.v, same name.
   Upstream: cat_uniq treats two sequences; allpairs_uniq_dep treats mapped
   dependent pairs using injectivity. The latter can express a more general
   route to this result, but no matching big-concatenation interface exists.
   Need: implementation/facts/job_constructor.v and
   analysis/facts/delay_propagation.v use this direct interface.
   Statement: unique index sequence, unique selected component sequences,
   and disjointness expressed by equality of indices sharing an element.
   Uses native big[cat/[::]] notation instead of Prosa's cat notation.
   Proof: structural induction, cat_uniq, mem_bigcatP, and disjointness.
   Decision: convenience candidate; if maintainers prefer allpairs_uniq_dep
   directly, retain a short Prosa wrapper instead. Further localization of
   the component hypotheses is possible and may be requested in review.

4. bigcat_nat_uniq
   Prosa: util/bigcat.v, same name.
   Upstream: no matching natural-range concatenation lemma. It follows
   from item 3 and iota_uniq; it adds no independent mathematical result.
   Need: implementation/facts/job_constructor.v and
   analysis/facts/behavior/arrivals.v use the range interface.
   Statement: natural interval, unique components, and the same disjointness
   hypothesis. Empty and reversed intervals are covered without extra bounds.
   Proof: item 3 and iota_uniq.
   Decision: propose with item 3; retain locally if upstream wants only the
   sequence lemma. The wrapper avoids exposing index_iota to clients.

PR C: sequence bounds for least common multiples (boot/bigop.v)
-------------------------------------------------------------
5. biglcmn_sup_seq
   Prosa: util/lcmseq.v, lcm_seq_is_mult_of_all_ints.
   Upstream: biglcmn_sup is indexed by a finType, while dvdn_biglcmP tests
   whether the entire LCM divides a number (the other direction).
   Neither is the proposed sequence bound.
   Need: analysis/facts/hyperperiod.v uses divisibility of each period into
   the sequence LCM in two places.
   Statement: generalized from a sequence of naturals to a filtered sequence
   of eqType indices and a nat-valued function. Any divisor of a selected
   value divides the big LCM. Prosa's foldr lcmn 1 follows via foldrE.
   Proof: big_rem, dvdn_lcml, dvdn_trans.

PR D: remainder comparison under equal quotients (boot/div.v)
------------------------------------------------------------
6. eqdivn_leqmodn
   Prosa: util/div_mod.v, same name.
   Upstream: divn_eq and leq_add2l derive it immediately; leq_mod only
   bounds a remainder by the original number. No equal-quotient comparison
   interface was found.
   Need: implementation/facts/extrapolated_arrival_curve.v uses the
   implication currently supplied by Prosa.
   Statement: strengthened to Boolean equality of the two order tests
   under equal quotients; no assumption d > 0. Division by zero is covered
   because divn_eq holds for zero as well.
   Proof: expand both dividends with divn_eq, rewrite equal quotients,
   and cancel their common prefix with leq_add2l.

Additional candidates from the complete four-file audit
------------------------------------------------------
7. bigcatE (PR B, boot/bigop.v)
   Convert filtered big concatenation to flatten of mapped filtered indices.
   No matching bridge is named upstream; foldrE and big_map prove it.
   This is the shared bridge behind the repeated membership conversions,
   not a proposal for separate interval/ordinal bridge variants.

8. mem_bigcatP (PR B, boot/bigop.v)
   Reflected membership view with index membership, predicate satisfaction,
   and component membership. Upstream flatten_mapP supplies the analogous
   flatten view, but no direct big-concatenation view exists.
   Consolidates mem_bigcat_nat, mem_bigcat_nat_exists, mem_bigcat, and
   mem_bigcat_exists; the unused ordinal variant should simply be deleted.
   Their 27 code occurrences justify one shared interface. The proposed
   view preserves the predicate witness that Prosa's exists form drops.

9. leq_sum_seq (PR F, boot/bigop.v)
   Support-local pointwise sum monotonicity. Existing leq_sum requires
   global selected-index bounds; big_seq_cond gives the short derivation.
   Prosa uses this interface 24 times, making it a useful API companion to
   the support-local predicate/count interfaces already proposed.

10. sum_nat_lt_size (PR F, boot/bigop.v)
    A nat-valued sum smaller than its support size implies has (F == 0).
    Generalizes sum_le_summation_range without interval arithmetic or
    specialized completion predicates. No matching zero-witness interface
    was found; leq_sum and sum1_size give the contrapositive proof.

11. leq_sum_partition (PR A, boot/bigop.v)
    Covering sequence of arbitrary eqType labels may contain duplicates;
    summing the partitions can overcount, yielding an inequality.
    Existing partition_big requires finite-type labels and decomposes an
    equality. Five uses of sum_over_partitions_le, and four of its
    excluded-label specialization reorder_summation, motivate one general
    sequence inequality rather than two client-specific lemmas.
    Proof: exchange_big, big_seq, leq_sum, and a positive label count.
    This proof is independent of the proposed PR F sum lemmas.

12. big_unit (PR G, boot/bigop.v)
    Generalize the 15-use sum_unit1 to an arbitrary monoid over unit indices.
    Upstream has big_ord1 and big_bool, but no unit-indexed counterpart.
    Proof: the unit enumeration is a singleton, then big_seq1.

13. sorted_filter_split (PR H, boot/path.v)
    A sorted sequence splits into the P and predC P filters when P is
    antitone for the transitive sorting relation. This generalizes the
    payload/threshold-specific sorted_split rather than encoding a local
    nat-valued function into an order on arbitrary payloads.
    Upstream ordered-type sorted_filter_le/gt are related, but do not
    directly cover a noninjective key function. Existing sorted_filter
    first handles Prosa's additional outer predicate; filter_predI then
    translates the two pieces. No matching generic relation/predicate
    decomposition was found. Proof uses path_sortedE and hasPn.
